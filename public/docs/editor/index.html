The song editor

The song editor

Every control in the Lyric Genie song editor, what it does, and where it sits.

The song editor is where you actually write. Open any song and this is what you get: a title, a page for your lyrics, and a ring of tools around the edge that you can mostly ignore until you need them.

This page is the map. Each tool has its own page, linked from here.

The toolbar#

Reading left to right.

Control What it does
Back Returns to wherever you came from. Your work is already saved.
Undo and Redo Step backwards and forwards through your edits.
Recordings Opens the audio attached to this song. See Recordings.
Record Starts recording straight away.
Freestyle Opens Freestyle ready but not recording.
Scratchpad Slides up a notepad that is not part of your lyrics.
Collaborators Invites co-writers and manages who is on the song.
More The menu described below.

The three audio buttons sit together in a grey capsule because they are three answers to the same question. Record captures audio. Freestyle captures audio and turns it into lyrics. Recordings is where both end up.

Tip

Freestyle opened from inside a song waits for you to press record. Opened from the Start tab it begins immediately. The difference is deliberate, and the Freestyle page explains why.

AI Context#

The bar under the toolbar reads Add AI context... until you fill it in. Tap it and it opens three fields.

Field The question it is really asking
Mood How is the song supposed to feel?
Theme What happens in this song, in a sentence?
Similar Artists Which artists inspire this song?

This is the single highest-leverage thing on the screen. Every writing tool in the app reads these three fields before it suggests anything, so a song with them filled in gets noticeably better results than an empty one. The app knows it too: open Wish Workshop on a song with no context and it stops to ask you for some before it will continue.

Theme does the most work, and it rewards being specific. "Leaving home" is weaker than "she leaves the town that made her small".

ProPro plan

Refine Context takes a rough idea and turns it into a filled-in mood, theme and artist list. It needs Pro. On the free plan, tapping it opens the upgrade screen.

The song title#

Type a title straight into it. The button beside it suggests titles based on what you have written, and you can page through the suggestions rather than taking the first one.

It stays greyed out until there are some lyrics to read, because a title suggestion with nothing to go on is just a random phrase.

The two floating buttons#

They sit over your lyrics at the bottom right, and they move. Press and drag either one up or down if it is covering a line you are working on.

The lamp opens Wish Workshop, which is the main AI writing tool. What it does depends on whether you have selected any text first. Select a line and it offers to change that line. Tap it with nothing selected and you get Creative Spark, which generates fresh material instead.

The grid opens Arrange mode, where you select whole lines and move, duplicate or delete them rather than editing text. Tap it again to leave.

The ⋯ menu#

Item What it does
Perform Full-screen scrolling lyrics on a black background, for singing along. Greyed out until the song has lyrics.
Lyric Sheet Creates a shareable web page of the finished lyrics with writer credits.
AI Style Standing instructions for the AI, including words you never want it to use.
Preferences App settings, including the serif lyric font and in-line suggestions.
Show Editor Tour Replays the guided tour of this screen. Useful, and not buried in Settings.

The Scratchpad#

The button slides up a ruled notepad from the bottom. It belongs to the song and it saves with the song, but it is not part of your lyrics, so nothing in it appears in a lyric sheet or in Perform.

Use it for the lines you are not sure about yet, rhyme lists, or a chorus you might come back to.

Note

Collaborators cannot see your Scratchpad. On a shared song it stays yours, which makes it the right place for a half-formed idea you are not ready to show anyone.

What changes on a shared song#

Invite a co-writer and the editor grows a few things.

  • A row of tabs appears, one per writer, each in their own colour, plus a Master tab for the agreed version.
  • A Mailbox button joins the toolbar, carrying a count when someone has sent you lines.
  • Collaborators moves off the toolbar and into the ⋯ menu.

Collaboration has its own section of these docs, starting with Inviting a co-writer.
