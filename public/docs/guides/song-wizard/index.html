Starting a song

The song wizard

A guided way to start a song. Answer three questions and Lyric Genie hands you a title and some phrases to begin from.

Starting at a blank page is the hardest part. The song wizard asks three questions, then generates a title and some phrases so there is something on the page before you begin.

Getting there#

From the Start tab, tap Write and choose Guided Start. The other option, Blank Editor, skips all of this and opens an empty song.

The three questions#

They are the same three fields as the AI Context bar in the song editor, asked one at a time. Whatever you answer is saved to the song, so filling them in here means not doing it later.

Each step offers suggestion chips, and More reshuffles them if none fit. Skip moves past any question you would rather not answer.

Step What it is asking How many
What's the mood? How the song should feel. Up to three.
What's the theme? What happens in it, in a sentence. One.
Similar Artists Who inspires the sound. Up to two.

Tip

Theme earns the most. "The more specific, the better the suggestions" is the app's own advice, and it is right. A sentence beats a word.

Then it sparks#

With your three answers in hand, the wizard generates ideas from them. This step is called Spark, and it comes in two parts.

Titles#

Eight at a time. Brainstorm More Titles replaces them with a new set from the same brief, so you can keep going until something lands. You do not have to take one.

Phrases#

These are fragments rather than finished lyrics: lost the plot again, wearing someone else's face, ghost of my own making. Tick the ones worth keeping and Add to Lyrics drops them into the song.

Tip

Take more than you need. A phrase you do not use costs nothing to delete, and the one that unlocks the song is rarely the one you expected.

Skip Spark leaves at any point, and you still get a song with your mood, theme and artists filled in.

The same idea, elsewhere#

Spark is not only in the wizard. Open Wish Workshop without selecting any text and you get Creative Spark, which does the same job for a song already under way.

There is also a tool that runs the other way round. Refine Context, the sparkle button on the AI Context bar, takes a rough idea in plain English and generates the mood, theme and artists from it. Spark turns a brief into ideas. Refine Context turns a feeling into the brief.

Two switches on that sheet decide how much of the song it is allowed to build on. Consider Existing Title lets it read the title you already have, and Consider Existing Context Fields lets it read whatever is already in the three fields. Leave both on to refine what you have. Turn both off to start over from the description alone.

Refine Context then offers you a set of concepts to choose between rather than overwriting anything. Pick one and tap Apply, or New Search to ask again.

ProPro plan

Spark and Refine Context both need AI access, so they are Pro features. See Plan limits.

What you end up with#

A song in the editor with a title, your three context fields, and any phrases you picked already in the lyrics. Nothing is locked in. The title can be changed, the phrases are ordinary lines you can delete.

If you skipped the title step, the song is called My Song until you rename it.
